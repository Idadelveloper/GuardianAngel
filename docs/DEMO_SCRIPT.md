# Until It Wasn't

**Guardian Angel — demo film · shooting script v2**

> Three women, three ordinary nights, and the thing none of them could reach for in time.

|              |                           |
|--------------|---------------------------|
| **Runtime**  | 2:58                      |
| **VO words** | ≈ 470                     |
| **Pace**     | ~2.3 words/sec            |
| **Format**   | 16:9 · 24fps              |
| **Cast**     | 3 on camera + 1 voiceover |

## Act structure

| Act | In   | Out  | Length |                                              |
|-----|------|------|--------|----------------------------------------------|
| 1   | 0:00 | 0:44 | 44s    | **Scenario** — three women                   |
| 2   | 0:44 | 1:02 | 18s    | **Problem** — how many of us there are       |
| 3   | 1:02 | 1:18 | 16s    | **Solution** — so we built her               |
| 4   | 1:18 | 2:14 | 56s    | **Demo** — what each of them would have done |
| 5   | 2:14 | 2:36 | 22s    | **Demo** — the night where nothing happens   |
| 6   | 2:36 | 2:51 | 15s    | Text me when you get in                      |
| —   | 2:51 | 2:58 | 7s     | Close                                        |

Acts 1–3 are a film. Acts 4–6 are a technical demo wearing the film's clothes — every
capability is named with the model or API behind it, in the same breath as the moment it
saves someone. Judges score what they can hear, so the stack is spoken, not supered.

# ACT 1 — Three women
**0:00 – 0:44 · Scenario**

> No music anywhere in this act. Diegetic sound only. The absence of a score is what makes
> these feel like footage rather than advertising — the moment a string pad comes in, the
> audience knows they are watching a pitch.

### 1A · 0:00 → 0:12 (12s)

`EXT. RESIDENTIAL STREET — NIGHT`

**VISUAL** — Handheld, three paces behind. **RACHEL** walks beside a man, relaxed,
mid-laugh. His hand settles on the small of her back. Stays. Slides lower. Her laugh stops.
She shifts away — his hand follows. *Hold on her face: she is not frightened yet, she is
calculating.* Freeze frame.

**CARD** — `RACHEL — 11:04 PM`

**VO** *(21 words)*
> This is Rachel. She's walking home from a date with someone she knows. It was going fine.
> Until it wasn't.

**SOUND** — Room tone, far-off traffic, two sets of footsteps. Her laugh cuts dead on the
freeze — hard gate, no tail. One struck low piano note under the card.

### 1B · 0:12 → 0:23 (11s)

`EXT. QUIET STREET — ANOTHER NIGHT`

**VISUAL** — Rachel alone. One earbud out. She glances back: a figure, half a block behind.
She walks faster. Glances again — still there. Without breaking stride she threads her keys
between her knuckles. *Never show the figure's face. We never learn whether he was
following her. Neither does she.* Freeze.

**CARD** — `RACHEL — AGAIN`

**VO** *(20 words)*
> This is Rachel again. Different night. She doesn't know if she's being followed. She only
> knows she's walking faster.

**SOUND** — Her breath high in the mix. Her footsteps, and a second set slightly out of
phase with them. Keys. Same piano note on the card.

### 1C · 0:23 → 0:35 (12s)

`INT. BAR — NIGHT`

**VISUAL** — Warm, busy, pleasant. **IDA**, one drink down, across from someone shot only
from behind. She laughs politely. Insert: her hand around the glass. The room tips a
degree. A rack focus goes soft and does not come back. She blinks hard. Looks at the glass.
Looks at the door. Freeze.

**CARD** — `IDA — 9:41 PM`

**VO** *(24 words)*
> This is Ida. One drink. Nothing has happened. She just doesn't feel right — and she can't
> tell if she's imagining it.

**SOUND** — Bar ambience, genuinely warm, then smeared — low-pass sweep, reverb tail
opening up, voices losing consonants. Her glass set down far too loudly.

### 1D · 0:35 → 0:44 (9s)

`BLACK`

**VISUAL** — Hold on black for a full beat before the VO starts. No text.

**VO** *(21 words)*
> None of them is in danger yet.
> That's the problem — by the time you're sure, you can't reach your phone.

**SOUND** — Everything drops out. One heartbeat, close-miked. Then nothing under the VO.

# ACT 2 — How many of us there are
**0:44 – 1:02 · Problem**

> Every figure is sourced at the foot of this document. Put the citation on screen in small
> type under each stat. A judge who can verify your number trusts the rest of your pitch.

### 2A · 0:44 → 1:02 (18s)

**VISUAL** — Black, with statistics set in plain mono type — one line at a time, straight
cuts, no animation. Between them, a slow drift through ordinary empty streets at night: a
bus shelter, an underpass, a car park, a front door. No people. *These are the places, and
they look like nothing.*

**CARDS** — `1 in 3 women · 840 million` → `~73% never reported` → `#IAmJaneDoe`

**VO** *(44 words — tight, keep the pace up)*
> One in three women alive today has survived physical or sexual violence. Eight hundred and
> forty million. Nearly three in four never report it — because they couldn't prove it
> happened. Last month a Cornell student filed as Jane Doe. Thousands answered: I am Jane
> Doe.

**SOUND** — Sub-bass drone, barely audible — felt more than heard. A dry mechanical tick as
each line of text cuts in. No music yet.

# ACT 3 — So we built her
**1:02 – 1:18 · Solution**

> The pivot. Music enters for the first time in the film, and it should feel like the
> lights coming on. The first technical claim lands here, on Angel's face, because the
> interface *is* the product for the first two seconds anyone uses it.

### 3A · 1:02 → 1:18 (16s)

**VISUAL** — Quick, almost subliminal cuts of the things women already do: a thumb sharing
a live location; keys in a fist; a text reading *"text me when you're home"*; a hand over a
drink. Then — a phone waking in the dark. Angel's face. She blinks. Hold on her.

**VO** *(38 words)*
> You already do this work. The shared location. The keys in your fist. You just don't have
> any backup.
>
> So we built her. Angel — drawn in Jetpack Compose, because an app you open when you're
> frightened should feel calm, not clinical.

**SOUND** — **Music in** on "So we built her" — felt piano, three or four notes, a warm pad
underneath. Nothing percussive yet. The montage cuts are silent; let the music carry them.

# ACT 4 — What each of them would have done
**1:18 – 2:14 · Demo**

> Each capability is paid off against a scene the audience has already watched, *and* named
> with the model behind it. Re-shoot the exact framing from Act 1 so recognition is instant.
> The VO carries the stack — the on-screen supers only reinforce it.

### 4A · 1:18 → 1:35 (17s) — Setup, voiceprint, privacy

`SETUP — SCREEN CAPTURE`

**VISUAL** — Fast, rhythmic cuts: permissions granted · voice enrolment with the waveform
moving · a guardian picked from contacts · a wake word typed, then spoken · the four
codeword tiers. Cut on the beat of the music.

**VO** *(41 words — slow very slightly through the model names)*
> Her wake word runs on sherpa-onnx keyword spotting — a three-million-parameter zipformer
> that takes any phrase you type, with no retraining. Setup also builds a CAM++ voiceprint:
> five hundred and twelve numbers, encrypted in the Android Keystore. Never your audio. So
> she wakes for you, and nobody else.

**SOUND** — Music gains a soft pulse at walking tempo — brushed, not electronic. UI sounds
almost inaudible: the product is discreet, so loud taps contradict the pitch.

### 4B · 1:35 → 1:49 (14s) — Scenario 1, the date

**VISUAL** — Same street, same framing. His hand moves. Rachel, without looking down,
without changing her tone: **"Hey Angel — pineapple."** Her phone stays in her bag. Cut to
the screen: recording running, transcript lines appearing, one labelled *Unfamiliar voice*.
Back to her face — nothing about it has changed.

**VO** *(34 words)*
> Rachel says one word. Moonshine Tiny transcribes it on the device, Silero gates the
> silence, and the speaker model labels every line — hers, or an unfamiliar voice. No sound.
> No screen. Nothing he can see.

**SOUND** — Deliberate silence where a confirmation chime would normally go. The audience
should notice that nothing happened.

### 4C · 1:49 → 2:01 (12s) — Scenario 2, followed

**VISUAL** — Rachel's thumb holds the SOS, phone low at her side. Screen: the alert goes
out, the guardian list ticks, a map link in the message. Cut to a second phone on a kitchen
counter lighting up with it.

**VO** *(29 words)*
> She holds the SOS. An agent decides who to wake, and sends one SMS with a live map link —
> then confirms it against the network. Sent means sent.

**SOUND** — One low, solid confirmation tone — the only assertive sound in the film. Then
the distant buzz of the second phone, diegetic, in another room.

### 4D · 2:01 → 2:14 (13s) — Scenario 3, the bar

**VISUAL** — Ida, barely moving her mouth: **"Hey Angel."** Her phone is face-down on the
table and stays face-down. Cut to the session afterwards: a timeline, key moments, a
summary written after the fact, lines attributed to an unfamiliar voice. Her thumb hovers
over *Delete* — and doesn't press it.

**VO** *(33 words)*
> For Ida, YAMNet tags what speech can't — a raised voice, an impact, breaking glass. When
> it ends, a summary agent writes the title and the key moments.
> Hers to delete. Or hers to keep.

**SOUND** — Music thins to piano alone for "Hers to delete. Or hers to keep." Let that line
sit in near-silence.

# ACT 5 — The night where nothing happens
**2:14 – 2:36 · Demo**

### 5A · 2:14 → 2:36 (22s)

`MAP — SCREEN CAPTURE`

**VISUAL** — The score at her front door reading 100. She steps outside; it begins to fall.
Pull back to the map: incident clusters carrying their own counts, safe havens, Angel
walking alongside her as the dot moves. Then a destination picked and two corridors
compared — safest against fastest.

**VO** *(51 words — the densest passage; land it cleanly)*
> But the best night is the one where nothing happens. The score blends Berkeley police and
> campus crime data, time of day, lighting and distance from home — a hundred at your door,
> falling the moment you leave. Google Maps Compose draws the hotspots. It's a deterministic
> scorer, not a black box, so every point is one you can question.

**SOUND** — Music opens up — add a sustained string or warm synth pad. This is the only
genuinely uplifting stretch in the film.

# ACT 6 — Text me when you get in
**2:36 – 2:51**

> The warmest scene in the film, and the one that should make someone's chest tighten a
> little. It is two friends saying goodbye on a doorstep.

### 6A · 2:36 → 2:51 (15s)

`EXT. DOORSTEP — NIGHT`

**VISUAL** — **VAISHALI** hugs **IDA** goodbye. Ida says it without thinking: *"Text me
when you get in."* On the pavement, Vaishali opens the map, picks the safest route, and
taps Angel's prompt — choosing Ida. She pockets the phone and walks. **Cut:** her front
door closing. **Cut:** Ida's phone, face-up on a table, lighting up by itself.

**CARD** — `Vaishali has arrived at Home at 11:52 PM`

**VO** *(36 words)*
> Vaishali picks safest over fastest. Then she picks Ida. A foreground service walks her
> home — locked screen, phone in a pocket — and the moment she's inside, Angel sends the
> text she'd have forgotten to send.

**SOUND** — Strip back to solo piano. On the arriving text: one real, unprocessed phone
notification tone — diegetic, slightly too loud in the quiet room. Then nothing.

**2:51 – 2:58**

# The stack, and where it is spoken

Everything claimed in the voiceover, with the beat it lands on. Nothing here is
aspirational — all of it runs in the build.

| Beat      | Spoken claim                                                      | What it actually is                                                                                                       |
|-----------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 3A · 1:02 | "Drawn in Jetpack Compose"                                        | Compose + Material 3, one custom theme, five-tier animated mascot on Canvas                                               |
| 4A · 1:18 | "sherpa-onnx keyword spotting, three-million-parameter zipformer" | Open-vocabulary KWS — any typed phrase registers at runtime, validated against the model vocabulary before it can be saved |
| 4A · 1:26 | "CAM++ voiceprint, 512 numbers, Android Keystore"                 | 512-d speaker embedding, AES-GCM encrypted at rest. The raw audio is never written to disk                                |
| 4B · 1:35 | "Moonshine Tiny transcribes on the device"                        | Streaming ASR via sherpa-onnx, RTF ≈ 0.05                                                                                 |
| 4B · 1:38 | "Silero gates the silence"                                        | VAD, so the transcriber only runs on actual speech                                                                        |
| 4B · 1:41 | "the speaker model labels every line"                             | Per-chunk attribution — *You* vs *Unfamiliar voice* — from the same CAM++ embedding                                       |
| 4C · 1:49 | "an agent decides who to wake"                                    | `GuardianNotificationAgent` — tiered escalation, de-duplication, never twice for one incident                             |
| 4C · 1:55 | "confirms it against the network"                                 | `PendingIntent` sent-receipts: reached / failed / unconfirmed are three different states                                  |
| 4D · 2:01 | "YAMNet tags what speech can't"                                   | 521 AudioSet classes via LiteRT; a tight danger subset drives escalation                                                  |
| 4D · 2:07 | "a summary agent writes the title and the key moments"            | Runs after the recording closes, so it can never delay an alert                                                           |
| 5A · 2:18 | "Berkeley police and campus crime data"                           | BPD + UCPD open data in 150 m privacy cells, plus NWS weather                                                             |
| 5A · 2:28 | "Google Maps Compose draws the hotspots"                          | maps-compose, lite-mode thumbnails, app-drawn pins                                                                        |
| 6A · 2:40 | "a foreground service walks her home"                             | `location`-typed FGS; the trip lives in Room so it survives the screen and the process                                    |

## The one claim to protect

**No audio ever leaves the phone.** Every model above runs on-device. It is the hardest
thing in the build and the only claim that separates Angel from every other panic-button
app. If a judge remembers one sentence, make it that one — consider supering it full-frame
at 1:33, between the voiceprint and the first scenario.

# Sound design

The whole film turns on one decision: **no music for the first forty-four seconds.**

## The motif

A single struck low piano note lands on each of the three freeze-frame cards. On the fourth
card it is replaced by a heartbeat — the pattern breaks exactly where the film does. The
same note returns, resolved rather than struck, over the final wordmark.

## Music cues

| Cue | In   | Character                                                              | Search terms                                   |
|-----|------|------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| M1  | 1:14 | Felt piano, three or four notes, warm pad. Enters on "So we built her." | minimal felt piano, intimate, hopeful          |
| M2  | 1:18 | Adds a brushed pulse at walking tempo under the demo.                  | soft cinematic underscore with light percussion |
| M3  | 2:14 | Opens out — sustained strings or warm synth pad over the map.          | uplifting ambient strings build                |
| M4  | 2:36 | Back to solo piano. Sparse. Ends unresolved until the title.           | sparse solo piano outro                        |

Uppbeat and the YouTube Audio Library are both safe for a hackathon submission. Epidemic
Sound and Artlist have far better material if anyone on the team already has a
subscription. Avoid anything with a drop.

## Rules for the mix

- **UI sounds stay near-inaudible.** The product's whole claim is that it is discreet. A
  satisfying tap sound every time the screen is touched argues against the pitch without
  anyone noticing why.
- **Two sounds are allowed to be assertive:** the alert confirmation at 1:49, and the
  arrival text landing at 2:47. Both are moments where something genuinely reached another
  person.
- **VO sits forward and dry.** Close mic, minimal reverb, no music ducking tricks — write
  the music so it never needs ducking.
- **The technical passages need more air, not more speed.** Model names are unfamiliar
  words; the read should slow very slightly through Act 4A rather than rushing to fit.

# Before you roll

## Four things that will bite you on the day

1. **The wake word has never been tuned against a real voice.** It is proven end to end
   through a loopback test, but nobody has yet said it aloud to an armed build and
   confirmed how it behaves across a room, through a bag, with background noise. *Film the
   spoken line and the screen capture separately and sync them in the edit.* Do not stake a
   live take on it.

2. **Grant `SEND_SMS` before filming.** Without it the alert opens a pre-filled composer
   instead of sending, and your screen capture contradicts the voiceover at 4C.
   Settings → Guardians → Allow sending texts.

3. **Test the arrival text against a real SIM first.** It is covered by unit tests but has
   not yet been fired end to end to a real phone. Use a teammate's number and warn them, or
   they will get a genuine "Ida has arrived" at midnight.

4. **Nothing on screen may imply that police were called.** The app does not dial emergency
   services and never claims to — the Emergency tier texts everyone and rings your first
   guardian. If a judge believes it auto-dials 911, that is a liability question you do not
   want in the Q&A.

## Capture settings

Record the screen off the device rather than filming it — it reads far cleaner.

```bash
adb shell screenrecord --bit-rate 16000000 --size 1080x2400 /sdcard/take.mp4
adb pull /sdcard/take.mp4
```

Turn off notifications and set Do Not Disturb before each capture, or someone's Snapchat
banner ends up in your pitch. The crime data is Berkeley-specific, so shoot the map
sequences there or caption them *Berkeley pilot*.

## Casting and consent

- Rachel's date and the figure following her are never seen clearly. That is a storytelling
  choice, not a shortcut — the film is about her experience, not about identifying a
  villain.
- Open with a short content warning card. It costs two seconds and it is the right thing to
  do for an audience that statistically includes survivors.
- Brief your cast on exactly what is being depicted before you shoot, and give anyone an
  unquestioned exit.

## If you need to cut to 2:30

Drop beat 1B (Rachel followed, 0:12–0:23) and its payoff at 4C, and trim Act 5 to twelve
seconds by cutting the route comparison. You keep two scenarios, the full technical
narration and the ending. **Cut the Vaishali scene last** — it is the only moment where the
product makes someone happy rather than safe, and it is what people will remember.

Do not cut 4A. Everything technical that follows depends on the audience already knowing
the models run on the phone.

# Sources for every figure quoted

- **1 in 3 women · 840 million** — WHO, *Lifetime toll: 840 million women faced partner or
  sexual violence*, 19 November 2025. 32% of women aged 15+, a figure that has fallen only
  ~0.2% a year since 2000.
- **Nearly three in four never reported** — RAINN: slightly more than 1 in 4 rapes and
  sexual assaults are reported to law enforcement.
- **Jane Doe v. Cornell University** — filed 16 September 2026; a 101-page complaint against
  seven members of the Xi chapter of Chi Phi, the university and others, over an alleged
  assault in October 2024. The *#IAmJaneDoe* solidarity campaign formed after she was doxxed
  and threatened. Reported by CNN, TIME and the Washington Post, October 2026.
- **Optional, if you want a fourth** — Gallup 2025: only 38% of women in the US say they
  feel safe walking alone at night in their own area.
